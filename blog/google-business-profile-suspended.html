Google Business Profile suspended? A contractor's guide to getting reinstated

Why Google suspends roofing, plumbing, HVAC and septic profiles, what to fix before you appeal, and the evidence that actually gets a profile reinstated.

By Ramjan Ali Akash7 min readPublished October 18, 2026

You search for your own business and it is gone from Google Maps. Or you log in and see a red banner saying your profile is suspended. For a roofer, plumber or HVAC company that gets most of its calls from the map pack, the phone goes quiet almost immediately.

The good news: most suspensions of real, legitimate contractors can be reversed. The bad news: the first reaction most owners have (editing everything, deleting the profile or making a new one) usually makes it worse. This guide walks through what to do, in order.

The short version: Do not delete the profile or create a new one. Find the guideline problem that most likely triggered the suspension, fix it, collect proof that your business is real and operates where you say it does, then submit one complete appeal.

What a suspension actually means

Google uses "suspended" for a few different situations, and they affect you differently. Check which one you are in before you do anything else.

What you seeWhat it usually meansImpact on calls
Profile missing from Maps and search, suspension notice in your dashboardGoogle believes the business, address or listing may not meet its guidelinesSevere. You get no calls from the profile until it is reinstated
Profile still visible, but you cannot edit it or reply to reviewsYour access or your account has been restricted, not necessarily the business itselfModerate. Calls continue, but you cannot manage the profile
Profile visible, with pending or rejected edits and a request to verifyA recent change (name, address, category) triggered a reviewLow to moderate, but can turn into a full suspension if ignored

If you manage several profiles from one Google account, also check whether the restriction is on the account (every profile affected) or just one listing.

Why contractor profiles get suspended

Service-area businesses, the trades that drive to the customer, are suspended far more often than shops and restaurants. They have no storefront for Google to check, and the industry has a long history of fake listings. These are the triggers I see most often, roughly in order.

TriggerWhy Google flags itFix before appealing
Keywords or city in the business name ("Best Roofing Dallas TX")Names must match the real-world brandChange it to your legal or signage name, exactly as on your license and trucks
Virtual office, P.O. box or coworking addressThe address cannot be verified as a real place of businessUse a real address you operate from, or hide the address and set a service area
Address shown when you do not serve customers thereService-area businesses that do not receive customers should hide the addressRemove the address from public view and list your service areas
Big edits in one go (name, address, category, phone)Several core changes at once look like a takeover or a rebrand to game rankingsMake one core change at a time, with evidence ready
Duplicate profiles for the same businessEach business should have one profile per real locationIdentify the duplicates and request removal of the extras
Service area that is far too bigAreas reaching well beyond a realistic drive look like an attempt to rank everywhereTrim the service area to where you really work

Website or phone mismatchTracking numbers or a site for a different brand create inconsistenciesMake the site and phone clearly match the business on the profile

"Your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers."

Google Business Profile guidelines, Guidelines for representing your business on Google

The name rule is the one contractors break most. It is tempting because keywords in a name can help rankings for a while. It is also the easiest thing for a competitor to report.

What to do in the first 24 hours

  1. Do not delete the profile. Deleting it can lose your reviews and history permanently.
  2. Do not create a new profile. A second listing for the same business looks like a duplicate and makes reinstatement harder.
  3. Stop making edits. Every edit while suspended adds noise to the review.
  4. Screenshot everything: the suspension notice, your current profile details, and any emails from Google.
  5. Read the notice carefully. It sometimes names the policy area. Then check your profile against Google's guidelines and the trigger table above.
  6. Keep the phone covered. While the profile is down, make sure your website, any Local Services Ads and other listings carry the right number so you lose as few calls as possible.

The evidence that gets profiles reinstated

An appeal is a request for Google to check that you are a real business operating as described. The more clearly you show that, the better. Prepare these before you submit:

EvidenceWhat it provesTips
Business or contractor licenseThe business legally exists and operates in the areaThe name and address should match the profile exactly
Utility bill or lease for the addressYou really operate from that addressRecent, showing the business name
Photos of branded vehicles and equipmentA real trade business, not a lead-generation listingShow the business name and phone on the truck
Photos of the location, including signageThe address is a real place of businessWide shot showing the street plus a close-up of the sign
Website with matching name, phone and servicesConsistency across your online presenceFix mismatches before you appeal, not after
Liability insurance certificateAn active, insured trade businessHelpful supporting evidence for service-area businesses

Google may also ask for a short video verification that shows your location, equipment and proof of management. If it does, record it in one continuous take: street signs or the outside of the building, then the branded vehicle and tools, then you opening the office or unlocking the equipment.

How to submit the appeal

  1. Fix the guideline problems you found first. Appealing with the same issues still in place usually gets a quick rejection.
  2. Open your profile while signed in and follow the link in the suspension notice to request a review. Google handles this through its Business Profile appeals process.
  3. Upload your evidence. Use clear, legible files, with the business name visible on every document.
  4. Keep the explanation short and factual: what the business is, where it operates, and what you corrected.
  5. Submit once and wait. Multiple appeals for the same issue do not speed anything up.

If the appeal is rejected: do not submit the same thing again. Work out what Google could not confirm (usually the address or the name), fix it or gather stronger proof, and then appeal again. The Business Profile Help Community can also escalate genuine cases.

After reinstatement: rankings and recovery

Once reinstated, most profiles get their reviews back and start showing again within days. Rankings can take a little longer to settle, and sometimes they do not fully return if the old rankings depended on the thing that was removed, such as keywords in the name.

That is normal. Rebuild with the signals that last: the right categories, a steady flow of reviews, a strong page on your website for each service, and the steps in how to rank in the Google map pack.

How to avoid another suspension

  • Use your real business name, with no added keywords or cities.
  • Hide your address if customers do not visit you, and keep the service area realistic.
  • Change one core detail at a time, and only when something genuinely changed.
  • Keep ownership in your own Google account and give agencies manager access only.
  • Ask every customer for a review, never only the happy ones, and never offer anything for it.
  • Keep your name, address and phone the same on your website and directories. The local SEO checklist for contractors covers this step by step.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Google take to review a suspension appeal?

Many appeals get an answer within a few business days, but some take several weeks, especially when Google asks for more evidence. Submitting complete evidence the first time is the best way to avoid delays.

Should I create a new Google Business Profile if mine is suspended?

No. A new profile for the same business is treated as a duplicate and usually makes things worse. It can also cut you off from the reviews and history attached to the original profile. Appeal the original instead.

Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?

Reviews are normally kept on the profile while it is suspended and come back when it is reinstated. They are at risk if you delete the profile or start a new one.

Can a competitor get my profile suspended?

A competitor can report your profile or suggest edits, and if the report points to a real guideline problem, such as keywords in your name or an address you do not really use, Google may act on it. A profile that follows the guidelines has very little to fear from reports.

Can you guarantee reinstatement?

No one can. Only Google decides. What I can do is find the likely trigger, fix it, and help you put together the evidence Google asks for, which gives a genuine business the best chance.
