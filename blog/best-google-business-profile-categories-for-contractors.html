Best Google Business Profile categories for roofers, plumbers, HVAC and septic companies

How to choose your primary and secondary Google Business Profile categories as a roofer, plumber, HVAC or septic company, and the mistakes that quietly hurt map rankings.

By Ramjan Ali Akash7 min readUpdated October 18, 2026

Your primary category is one of the strongest signals in your Google Business Profile. It tells Google what kind of business you are, and it heavily influences which searches you can show up for in the map pack. A wrong category is one of the most common reasons a good contractor stays invisible.

This guide explains how to choose categories, with examples for the trades I work with most. Google adds, renames and retires categories from time to time, so always confirm the exact wording in the category picker inside your own profile.

How categories work

  • One primary category. This is the most important one. Choose the one that matches the work that makes you the most money and that you most want to be found for.
  • Several secondary categories. These describe other things you do. They help you appear for related searches but carry less weight than the primary.
  • Categories are not keywords. You pick from Google's list. You cannot invent one, and you should not stuff the list with loosely related options.

Examples by trade

These are common starting points, not rules. Your own mix of services should decide the final choice.

Roofing companies

  • Primary: Roofing contractor
  • Possible secondary: Roof repair service, Metal roofing contractor or Siding contractor, only if you genuinely offer them

Plumbers

  • Primary: Plumber
  • Possible secondary: Water heater installation service, Drainage service or Emergency plumber, where they match real offerings

HVAC companies

  • Primary: HVAC contractor
  • Possible secondary: Air conditioning contractor, Heating contractor, Air conditioning repair service

Septic companies

  • Primary: Septic system service
  • Possible secondary: Septic tank cleaning service or similar categories that fit your actual work

Check the picker. Category names change. Type your service into the category field in your profile and see what Google offers right now, rather than copying a list from any article, including this one.

How to choose your primary category

  1. Start with revenue. Which service do you most want more of? Roof replacement? Water heater installs? Choose the category that fits it best.
  2. Look at who ranks. Search your main service in your city and open the profiles of the top three map results. Note their primary categories. Many tools and some browser extensions can show them.
  3. Prefer the specific honest match. If you do mostly one thing, pick the category for that thing, not a broader one you hope will catch more.
  4. Keep the website consistent. The services on your site should match your categories.

Mistakes that hurt

  • Choosing a vague category. A very general primary can keep you out of the narrower searches that bring in booked jobs.
  • Adding every category you can think of. Unrelated categories confuse what the business is about.
  • Copying a competitor blindly. They may be wrong, or offer something you do not.
  • Changing categories often. Make one reasoned change, then give it a few weeks before judging.
  • Offering a category you do not serve. It can attract the wrong calls and, in some cases, create compliance problems.

Fill in what categories unlock

After choosing categories, finish the parts of the profile that depend on them:

  • Services list: add each service you offer, with a short description.
  • Description: clear, natural, no keyword stuffing.
  • Service areas: the towns you actually serve.
  • Photos: real jobs, team and trucks.

Make changes carefully

Change the primary category deliberately, and write down what it was before so you can compare results. A good change can show movement in the map within a few weeks, though results vary with competition (see how long local SEO takes).
