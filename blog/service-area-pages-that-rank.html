How to write service area pages that rank (without doorway spam)

How home-service businesses can create location pages for the towns they serve that are genuinely useful, rank well and stay clear of Google's doorway rules.

By Ramjan Ali Akash7 min readUpdated October 1, 2026

Service area pages, sometimes called location pages or city pages, are pages on your website about the specific towns you serve. Done well, they help you rank in places further from your office. Done badly, they are one of the fastest ways to fill a site with pages Google ignores.

Why most city pages fail

The classic approach is a template: "Best Plumber in [City]", the same few paragraphs, and the city name swapped in fifty times. Google's spam policies specifically call out doorway pages, which are pages created to rank for similar searches that lead to essentially the same content.

The test is simple. If you could swap the town name and the page would still be true, it is not a real local page.

Only create pages for towns that matter

Start with a short list:

  • Towns where you already do a lot of work
  • Towns where you want more work and can reach quickly
  • Towns with enough searches to be worth targeting

Five excellent pages beat fifty thin ones. You can always add more once the first ones rank.

What to put on a service area page

Real jobs in that town

This is the most valuable content you have. A short write-up of a few jobs: what the problem was, what you did, and photos. "Replaced a 20-year-old furnace in a 1970s ranch in Plano" is local content no competitor can copy.

Reviews from customers there

Feature reviews from customers in that town. They are proof that you really work there.

Local details that matter for the job

Think about what is genuinely different about working in that area:

  • Typical housing age and what that means for plumbing, roofs or HVAC
  • Soil and water conditions (very relevant for septic and plumbing)
  • Weather patterns: hail, freezes, humidity, hurricanes
  • Permit or inspection requirements, linked to the official local source

Practical service information

  • Which services you offer in that area
  • Typical response times
  • Neighborhoods and ZIP codes you cover
  • Your phone number and a simple way to book

Links to your service pages

Each area page should link to your main service pages, and the most important service pages should link back to your top area pages.

Page titles and headings

Keep them natural. A title like "Roof Repair & Replacement in Katy, TX | Your Company" is clear for both searchers and Google. Avoid stuffing several towns and services into one title.

Service area pages and the map pack

Area pages mainly help you rank in regular organic results for towns further from your office. The map pack still leans heavily on distance from your verified address. Both together give you the widest reach.

Rule of thumb: If a homeowner in that town would find the page useful even without wanting to hire you today, you are on the right track.

Keep them updated

Add new jobs and reviews over time. A page that keeps growing with real local work keeps getting stronger.
