Local Services Ads vs SEO for contractors: which brings cheaper calls?

How Google Local Services Ads, Google Ads and local SEO compare for roofers, plumbers, HVAC and septic companies, and how to decide where your marketing budget goes.

By Ramjan Ali Akash6 min readPublished October 18, 2026

Search "plumber near me" and you will usually see three things before any normal website: Local Services Ads with a "Google Screened" or "Google Guaranteed" badge, regular Google Ads, and the map pack. Each one is a different way to pay for calls, and contractors often spend on the wrong one first.

This guide compares them honestly, including where SEO is not the right answer yet.

The short version: Local Services Ads and Google Ads buy calls now, at a price that never really goes down. Local SEO costs more up front relative to the calls it brings in the early months, then the cost per call keeps falling while the rankings hold. Most established contractors do best with both: ads for speed, SEO for margin.

How Local Services Ads work

Local Services Ads are Google's pay-per-lead ads for service businesses. To run them, your business goes through Google's screening, which for most trades includes background checks and license and insurance verification. Once approved, you appear with a badge at the very top of many service searches, and customers can call or message you directly.

You do not choose keywords. You choose your job types, service area, hours and weekly budget. Where you appear depends on things like your proximity to the customer, your reviews, how quickly you answer, your hours and your budget.

Where Local Services Ads shine

  • A new business, or one with few reviews, that needs calls now.
  • Emergency trades where the top phone number on the page wins.
  • Filling slow weeks without a long commitment.

Where they hurt

  • You pay for every lead, including tire-kickers and wrong job types. You can dispute some invalid leads, but not all.
  • Lead prices tend to rise as more competitors in your area join.
  • You build nothing you keep. The day the budget stops, so do the calls.

How local SEO works by comparison

Local SEO is the work that gets your Google Business Profile into the map pack and your website into organic results: profile optimization, service pages, service area pages, reviews, citations and local links.

"Local results are based primarily on relevance, distance, and prominence."

Google, Tips to improve your local ranking on Google

You cannot buy any of those three. That is the downside, because it takes time, and the upside, because once you have them a competitor cannot simply outbid you tomorrow.

Running both without wasting money

  • Track calls by source. Use a separate tracking number for each channel so you know what each booked job actually cost.
  • Use the same reviews strategy for both. More recent, detailed reviews help your profile rank and help your ads convert.
  • Turn ads down as rankings grow. When you rank in the map pack for a service, test lowering the ad budget for that service and watch total calls.
  • Answer fast. Responsiveness affects Local Services Ads, and a missed call from either channel is money gone.

Frequently asked questions

Do Local Services Ads help my SEO or map rankings?

Not directly. Local Services Ads are a separate paid placement and do not change how your Google Business Profile ranks in the map pack. They share some ingredients, such as reviews and accurate business details, so work on one often helps the other.

What does a Local Services Ads lead cost?

It depends on the trade, the job type and the market. Prices range from tens of dollars for some services to well over $100 for competitive trades such as roofing in large cities. Google shows the expected cost per lead in your account when you set up.

Can I stop paying for SEO once I rank?

You can scale it down, but rankings need upkeep. Competitors keep working, reviews need to stay recent and pages need updating. Most businesses move to a lighter maintenance plan rather than stopping completely.

Which is better for emergency jobs like burst pipes or no-heat calls?

Local Services Ads and the map pack both do well for urgent searches because they show a phone number at the top of the page. In practice, the strongest setup for emergency trades is a well-ranked Google Business Profile plus Local Services Ads to cover the gaps.

Is SEO worth it for a small, one-truck business?

Often yes, especially in smaller towns and less crowded trades where the map pack is easier to win. A free audit is the quickest way to see how hard your market is before you spend anything.
