Septic SEO: 16 keywords septic companies should target

The septic pumping, inspection and installation keywords worth targeting, how to group them into pages, and which ones bring calls rather than just clicks.

By Ramjan Ali Akash7 min readUpdated October 18, 2026

Septic is one of the best niches in local SEO. Searches are urgent or tied to a real deadline, customers rarely shop around for long, and many competitors have thin websites and a half-finished Google Business Profile. That gap is an opening, but only if you target the right searches with the right pages.

Below are the keyword groups I would build around for a septic company, what the searcher actually wants, and where each one belongs on your site.

1. Emergency and high-intent keywords

These searchers have a problem right now and will usually call the first credible result.

  • septic tank pumping near me
  • septic pumping [city]
  • septic backup and septic tank overflowing
  • emergency septic service [city]

The "near me" and city versions are decided mostly by the map pack, so your Google Business Profile matters as much as your page. See how to rank in the Google map pack.

2. Scheduled-service keywords

People searching these are planning, which means they compare a little more and read reviews.

  • how often to pump a septic tank (information, but it leads straight to a booking)
  • septic tank cleaning cost
  • septic maintenance plan

3. Inspection keywords tied to a home sale

These are often overlooked and tend to be less competitive than pumping.

  • septic inspection for home sale
  • septic inspection near me
  • septic certification [county], if your area requires it

Buyers and agents search these on a deadline. A clear page that explains what the inspection includes, how long it takes and how fast you can schedule it converts well.

4. Installation and repair keywords

Fewer searches, but each one is worth far more than a routine pump-out.

  • septic system installation [city]
  • septic tank replacement cost
  • drain field repair and drain field replacement
  • septic perc test, where it applies to your area

How to turn the list into pages

Do not put every keyword on one page. Give each distinct job its own page, and keep one clear purpose per page:

  • A pumping and cleaning page
  • An inspection page
  • An installation and replacement page
  • A drain field repair page
  • A service area page for each town you want calls from, with real local detail

For the town pages, avoid copy-and-paste text with the city name swapped. Service area pages that rank explains how to make them useful instead of spammy.

What to include on every page

  • Your phone number and a call button visible without scrolling
  • The areas you serve, written as real place names
  • What the service involves, how long it takes and what affects the price
  • Photos of your own trucks and crew
  • A few reviews from customers in that area

Prioritize by value, not search volume

A keyword with 20 searches a month for "septic tank replacement" can be worth more than 500 searches for a general question, because one job pays for months of work. Start with the pages closest to a booked job, then add educational posts that feed them.

A tool can give rough volume, but the better check is your own Search Console: the queries that already show impressions without clicks are the easiest wins.

Common mistakes

  • One "Services" page that lists everything and ranks for nothing
  • Targeting the whole state instead of the towns you actually serve
  • Ignoring the Google Business Profile, where most urgent searches are decided
  • No page for inspections, even though home-sale searches are steady
