How to get more septic pumping customers from Google: a 90-day plan

A practical 90-day plan for septic pumping, inspection and install companies to get more calls from Google Maps and search, across every town they drive to.

By Ramjan Ali Akash6 min readPublished October 18, 2026

Septic is one of the best trades for local SEO. Fewer companies compete online than in plumbing or roofing, every home on septic needs regular pumping, and urgent problems turn into calls fast.

The challenge is geography. Your customers are spread across counties and small towns, often 30 or 40 minutes from your yard, and Google does not automatically connect your business to those places. This plan fixes that in 90 days.

In this article

  2. The searches worth winning

  4. Common mistakes that cost septic companies calls

The short version: Fix your Google Business Profile and service area, build one strong page per septic service, add useful pages for the counties that bring in real work, collect reviews from across the whole area, and own the real estate inspection searches.

Why septic search is different

  • The demand repeats. Every home on septic needs regular pumping, so a customer you win once can come back every few years.
  • The service area is huge. You are often competing town by town against small local operators, not one dominant company.
  • Urgent searches convert. "Septic backing up" or "sewage smell in yard" is someone who needs a truck today.
  • Real estate drives demand. Home sales often need a septic inspection, with a deadline attached.

"Household septic tanks are typically pumped every three to five years."

U.S. EPA, How to Care for Your Septic System

That cycle matters for marketing. Every pumping job is a customer you can remind in three years, and every new homeowner in your area is a future customer who will probably start with a Google search.

Weeks 1 to 2: profile, service area and tracking

  • Set "Septic system service" as the primary category, and add secondary categories only for work you really do.
  • If customers do not come to your yard, hide the address and list the towns and counties you serve. Keep it realistic; an area far bigger than you can actually cover can trigger a review. See why contractor profiles get suspended.
  • List every service in the profile: pumping, inspections, real estate inspections, repairs, drain fields, installs, grease traps.
  • Add real photos: your trucks, crew, equipment and jobs.
  • Set up call tracking so you know which towns and services the calls come from.

Weeks 3 to 6: service pages and reviews

A single "Services" page with a bullet list will not rank for six different kinds of search. Build one page per service, each answering what the customer actually wants to know: what the job involves, how long it takes, signs you need it, what affects the price, and which areas you cover. The content SEO approach I use starts with exactly this structure.

At the same time, start a review routine:

  • Ask on site, the same day, and text a direct review link before leaving.
  • Ask customers to mention the service and their town in their own words.
  • Ask every customer, not only the ones you expect to be happy, and never offer anything in return. Google's review policies forbid both.
  • Reply to every review, including the bad ones.

Weeks 7 to 10: county and town pages

This is where septic companies win the outer parts of their service area. Build pages for the counties and towns that bring in real work, and make each one genuinely local:

  • Soil and site conditions that affect systems there.
  • Permit and inspection rules for that county.
  • Common system types (conventional, aerobic, mound) in the area.
  • Real jobs you have done there, with photos.

Pages that only swap the town name are the fastest way to waste this step. How to write service area pages that rank covers how to avoid it. Clean up your citations at the same time so your name, address and phone match everywhere.

Weeks 11 to 13: real estate partners and local links

  • Create a clear real estate inspection page: what is checked, turnaround time and what the report includes.
  • Introduce yourself to local agents and home inspectors. A page they can send clients to makes referring you easy.
  • Earn local links from county extension offices, local business associations, sponsorships and supplier directories. Local link building matters more in rural markets than most owners expect.
  • Review what came in: calls by town and service, rankings across the area, and which pages bring leads.

How to measure whether it is working

  • Map rankings checked across a grid of your service area, not just from your yard.
  • Calls and form leads by town and by service.
  • Inspection requests and agent referrals.
  • Review count and how recent the latest reviews are.

If you work in states where septic is common outside the cities, such as North Carolina or Georgia, the opportunity is often bigger than owners expect. Frequently asked questions

What Google Business Profile category should a septic company use?

Most septic companies should use “Septic system service” as the primary category. Add secondary categories only for work you genuinely do, for example drainage or excavation services. Check which categories the top three competitors in your area use.

Should I build a page for every town I serve?

No. Build pages for the counties and towns that bring in real work, and give each one genuinely local detail such as soil, permit rules and typical systems. Thin pages that only swap the town name can be treated as doorway pages.

How long does SEO take for a septic company?

Septic is usually less competitive online than plumbing or roofing, so profile fixes and service pages often show results faster. Many septic companies see movement in the first two to three months, with the outer towns in the service area following over the next few months.

Is it worth targeting real estate inspection searches?

Yes. Inspection searches come with a deadline, so buyers and agents call quickly, and a good inspection experience often turns into a long-term pumping customer and agent referrals.

How do I get reviews from customers in remote towns?

Ask on site, the same day, and send a direct review link by text before you leave. Ask customers to mention the service and the town in their own words. Never offer a discount or gift for a review.
