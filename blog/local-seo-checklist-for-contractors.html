Local SEO checklist for contractors

A practical, step-by-step local SEO checklist for roofers, plumbers, HVAC and septic companies. Check each item yourself, in order.

By Ramjan Ali Akash8 min readUpdated October 1, 2026

Most contractors do not need a complicated SEO strategy. They need the basics done properly, in the right order, and kept up every month. This checklist is the order I work through for every new home-service client.

Go through it honestly. Every unchecked item is a reason a competitor is getting the call instead of you.

1. Google Business Profile basics

Your Google Business Profile is the single most important local SEO asset you have. It is what shows up in the map pack.

  • Verified and owned by you. The profile should be under your own Google account, not a former marketing agency's.
  • The right primary category. This is one of the strongest relevance signals. "Roofing contractor", "Plumber", "HVAC contractor" and "Septic system service" are common choices. Pick the one that matches your main money-maker.
  • Relevant secondary categories. Add the other services you genuinely offer, without stretching.
  • Business name as it really is. Do not add keywords or city names to your name. It breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended.
  • Service area set correctly. If customers do not come to you, hide your address and list the areas you serve.
  • Accurate hours. Only say 24 hours if someone really answers the phone.
  • Services listed in full. Every service you want calls for, with a short description.
  • Real photos. Your trucks, your crew, your finished jobs. Add new ones regularly.

2. Reviews

Reviews affect both rankings and whether people call. The aim is a steady flow, not a one-time push.

  • Ask every happy customer, ideally the same day the job is finished, with a direct review link sent by text.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad, calmly and specifically.
  • Never offer discounts or gifts for reviews, and never ask only the customers you expect to be happy. Both break Google's rules.
  • Never buy or write fake reviews. Beyond Google removing them, the FTC's rule on fake reviews allows penalties against businesses that use them.

3. Your website

  • One page per core service. Not one "Services" page with a bullet list. Each important service needs its own page.
  • Pages for your main service areas, with real local content, not the same text with a different town name.
  • Name, address and phone on the site, matching your Google profile exactly.
  • Fast on phones. Most of your visitors are on mobile, often in a hurry.
  • Click-to-call visible at the top of every page.
  • LocalBusiness schema with your business details, so Google can read them reliably.
  • Search Console set up, so you can see which searches bring visitors and spot errors.

4. Citations

Citations are listings of your business name, address and phone number on other websites: directories, data aggregators and industry sites.

  • Make sure your details are identical everywhere. Old phone numbers and old addresses confuse Google.
  • Cover the major ones first, then trade-specific directories and local ones like your chamber of commerce.

5. Local links

Links from other local and relevant websites build prominence. The best ones usually come from relationships you already have:

  • Suppliers and manufacturers with dealer or installer listings
  • Trade associations you belong to
  • Local teams, events or charities you sponsor
  • Builders, realtors and other businesses you work with

6. Tracking

If you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether SEO is paying off.

  • Track calls and form submissions from your website and your Google profile.
  • Track map rankings across your service area, not just from your office address.
  • Ask every new customer how they found you, and write it down.

Where to start: If you only have time for one thing this week, fix your primary category and set up a simple review request after every job. Those two alone move a lot of businesses.

Keep it going

Local SEO is not a one-time project. Competitors keep adding reviews, photos and pages. The businesses that hold the top spots are the ones that keep doing the basics every month.
